ShahiPaneer

Shahi Paneer is a rich, royal vegetarian curry from the Indian subcontinent made with soft paneer cheese cubes in a thick, velvety gravy.

  • Paneer: 200 to 250 grams, cut into cubes or triangles
  • Base Veggies: 2 medium onions (chopped) and 3 to 4 medium tomatoes (chopped)
  • Nuts: 10 to 12 cashew nuts (soaked or boiled for creaminess)
  • Aromatics: 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste or 1 inch ginger and 4 cloves garific
  • Whole Spices: 1 bay leaf, 1-inch cinnamon stick, 2 green cardamons, 1 black cardamom, and 2-4 cloves
  • Cooking Fat: 2 tablespoons oil and 2 tablespoons butter (or ghee)
  • Spices: ½ teaspoon turmeric powder, 1 teaspoon red chili powder (or Kashmiri chili), 1 teaspoon coriander powder, and ½ teaspoon garam masala
  • Dairy & Extras: ¼ cup plain yogurt (curd, whisked), ¼ cup heavy cream, 1 teaspoon sugar, and salt to taste
  • Finishing Touches: 2 teaspoons crushed kasuri methi (dried fenugreek leaves) and a pinch of saffron (kesar)
  1. Sauté the Base: Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a pan. Add the whole spices and let them crackle.
  2. Cook Aromatics and Veggies: Add chopped ginger, garlic, and onions. Sauté until onions turn golden brown, then add tomatoes and cashews. Cook until the tomatoes turn soft.
  3. Blend and Strain: Cool the mixture completely, add the yogurt, and blend into a smooth paste. Strain the puree through a sieve for a silky, velvety texture.
  4. Make the Masala: In a separate kadai (wok), heat 2 tablespoons of butter and oil. Add turmeric, chili powder, coriander powder, and cumin powder on low heat until aromatic.
  5. Simmer the Gravy: Pour in the sieved tomato-cashew puree and heavy cream. Add salt and sugar, then cook until the oil begins to separate from the edges.
  6. Add Paneer: Gently drop in the paneer cubes, saffron strands, and a splash of water or milk to adjust the thickness. Simmer for 5 minutes.
  7. Garnish and Serve: Crush the kasuri methi between your palms and stir it in along with fresh coriander leaves. Serve hot with naan or roti.
